First, how big is a meter and a second?

  • 1 meter (m) is about one big step, or about half the height of a standard door (a door is about 2 m high). In feet, 1 m is about 3.3 ft, or 39 inches.
    • An Alto-size car is about 3.5 m long (an Alto K10 is 3.545 m), so about 3 to 4 big steps.
    • A two-storey house is about 6 to 7 m tall.
  • 1 second (s) is one tick of a wall clock’s second hand. It is also about one heartbeat of a person resting (a resting adult beats 60 to 100 times a minute, so roughly once a second). Counting “ek, do, teen” at a calm, steady pace is also roughly one count per second.
  • 1 kilogram (kg) is a 1 kg packet of atta or sugar, or a 1 litre bottle of water. In pounds, it is a bit over 2 lb (2.2 lb).
  • Speed is how many meters you cover in 1 second. 1 m/s is a slow walk. A normal walking pace is about 1.4 m/s, which is 5 km/h or 3 mph. To convert, multiply m/s by 3.6 to get km/h.
    • A city bus in traffic: 20 to 30 km/h, which is 6 to 8 m/s (12 to 19 mph).
    • An auto-rickshaw cruising: 30 to 40 km/h, which is 8 to 11 m/s (19 to 25 mph).
    • A bike at 60 km/h is 17 m/s (37 mph).
  • Everything below is built from these ideas.

How much is m/s^2

Start with a car. An Alto K10 takes about 12 to 15 seconds to go from 0 to 100 km/h (62 mph) in road tests. Say 13 seconds. It gains about 2 m/s of speed every second. That is an acceleration of about 2 m/s^2.

  • 100 km/h = 27.8 m/s, so 27.8 m/s / 13 s = 2.1 m/s^2.
  • A quicker car, one that does 0 to 100 km/h in 10 seconds, gains 2.8 m/s every second, so about 2.8 m/s^2.
  • 1 m/s^2 means your speed goes up by 1 m/s (about a slow walk) in every second.
  • Formula: acceleration = change in speed / time

Gravity is also an acceleration. On Earth it is 9.8 m/s^2.

  • Step off a 1 m ledge (a big step down, about half a door’s height). Gravity speeds you up for about half a second, and you land at about 4.4 m/s, which is roughly 16 km/h or 10 mph. That is the jolt in your knees.
  • After 1 second of falling, your speed is 9.8 m/s (35 km/h, 22 mph). That is an auto-rickshaw’s cruising speed, reached in one second. After 2 seconds, it is 19.6 m/s (71 km/h, 44 mph).
  • Falling off a two-storey roof (about 6.5 m) takes about 1.2 seconds, and you hit the ground at about 11 m/s, which is roughly 40 km/h or 25 mph.
  • After 10 seconds of falling, your speed would be 9.8 x 10 = 98 m/s (353 km/h, 219 mph). In that time you would have fallen about 490 m (1,600 ft), which is nearly 7 Qutub Minars stacked up: distance = 1/2 x 9.8 x 10^2 = 490 m.
  • That 98 m/s is only true with no air. In real life, air drag stops a falling person from getting faster at about 55 m/s (200 km/h, roughly 120 mph) when falling belly-down. This is why skydivers don’t keep speeding up.
  • For comparison, Usain Bolt’s top speed is about 12 m/s (43 km/h, 27 mph), which for a moment is about an auto-rickshaw’s cruising speed.

How much is 1 Newton

Hold a 100 g apple (about 3.5 oz) in your hand. The pull of gravity on it, the force you feel in your palm, is about 1 Newton (N).

  • Formula: force = mass x acceleration
  • 1 N is the force needed to speed up a 1 kg mass by 1 m/s every second (1 N = 1 kg x 1 m/s^2).
  • Apple: 0.1 kg x 9.8 m/s^2 = 0.98 N, so about 1 N. In pounds-force, 1 N is about 0.22 lbf.
  • A 1 kg packet of atta pulls down with about 9.8 N, so about 10 N.
  • A 60 kg (132 lb) person: 60 kg x 9.8 m/s^2 = 588 N. That is the force Earth pulls them down with, and the force they press on the floor with while standing still (about 132 lbf).

How much is 1 Joule

A Joule is the energy it takes to push with a force of 1 N over a distance of 1 m.

  • Formula: energy (work) = force x distance, so 1 J = 1 N x 1 m.
  • Lift that 100 g apple from the floor up to a table about 1 m (3.3 ft) high. You pushed with about 1 N over 1 m, so you spent about 1 J.
    • 0.1 kg x 9.8 m/s^2 x 1 m = 0.98 J
  • Lifting the 1 kg packet of atta by 1 m takes about 9.8 J.
  • A typical phone battery (about 3,000 mAh) holds roughly 11 Wh, which is about 40,000 J. That is the same energy as lifting around 40,000 apples up by 1 m.

How much is 1 Watt

A Watt is how fast you use energy: 1 Watt = 1 Joule every second.

  • Formula: power = energy / time, so 1 W = 1 J / 1 s.
  • Same apple, lifted 1 m (3.3 ft) in 2 seconds:
    • Energy = 0.1 kg x 9.8 m/s^2 x 1 m = 0.98 J
    • Power = 0.98 J / 2 s = 0.49 W
    • So you need about half a Watt to lift that apple in 2 seconds.
  • Some everyday powers:
    • A person resting gives off about 100 W of heat. That is why a crowded room gets warm.
    • A basic phone charger (5 V, 2 A) delivers about 10 W.
    • A ceiling fan uses about 70 W (a newer energy-saving BLDC fan uses less, about 30 W).
    • An electric kettle is about 1500 to 2000 W, and a geyser (water heater) is typically about 2000 W.
    • A room air conditioner (AC) draws about 1500 to 2000 W while its compressor is running.
  • A 100 W light bulb uses 100 J every second (100 W = 100 J/s). That is the old filament kind. A modern LED bulb that lights a room about as well as a 60 W filament bulb uses only about 9 W.

What is Watt-hour

A Watt-hour (Wh) is a unit of energy, not power. It is the energy used by 1 Watt running for 1 hour.

  • Formula: energy = power x time
  • 1 Wh = 1 W x 1 h = 3,600 J
  • Example, a 100 W light bulb running for 1 hour:
    • Energy = 100 W x 1 h = 100 Wh = 0.1 kWh (360,000 J)
  • Example, an AC drawing 2000 W (power) running for 1 hour:
    • Energy = 2000 W x 1 h = 2000 Wh = 2 kWh = 2 units

What is kilowatt-hour

  • 1 kWh is 1000 Wh (3,600,000 J).
  • It is the common unit for electricity. In India it is called one “unit” on your bill.
  • An AC drawing 2 kW (2000 W) uses 2 kWh of energy for every hour it runs, which is 2 units.
  • What does 1 unit (1 kWh) buy you?
    • A 100 W bulb for 10 hours.
    • A ceiling fan (about 70 W) for roughly 14 hours.
    • A 2000 W geyser or kettle for 30 minutes.
    • An AC at full power (1500 to 2000 W) for about 30 to 40 minutes. An inverter AC averaging 1000 W (because its compressor cycles on and off) would run for about 1 hour.
    • A phone charger for about 100 hours, or about 90 full charges of a 11 Wh phone battery.
  • Rough cost (indicative only): in 2025-26, domestic electricity in most Indian states works out to about Rs 3 to Rs 10 per unit at typical household use. It varies by state, slab and subsidy, and some states charge less or more. Check your own bill. At that range, a 100 W bulb for 10 hours costs about Rs 3 to Rs 10, and an AC at 2 kW for 1 hour costs about Rs 6 to Rs 20.
